Watch the branches grow.

The timeline records discovery, publication, influence, correction, and formalization. It is intentionally separate from QEVA's logical dependency graph: the historical order in which humans found ideas is not necessarily the shortest logical path between them.

What the final QEVA history map must contain

A complete historical graph needs more than famous milestones. It must ingest works and metadata at scale, reconcile identifiers and dates, record uncertainty and disputed priority, preserve citations, and distinguish introduction, proof, independent discovery, refutation, generalization, and later influence.

Current status: this page is a deliberately small seed illustrating the data model. It is not presented as a complete or neutral history of mathematics.
