EXECUTABLE INTUITION

Perturb the machinery.

These experiments are deliberately small. Their purpose is not to replace proof; it is to make the mechanism visible and expose which assumptions control the behavior.

RECURRENCE → SENSITIVITY

How repeated rules can amplify small differences

x[n+1] = r x[n] (1 − x[n])

Run two logistic-map trajectories separated initially by Δ. The same recurrence can converge, cycle, or exhibit sensitive behavior depending on r. Recurrence itself does not imply chaos.

Interpretation constraint: visual separation is evidence about this numerical experiment, not by itself a proof of a formal chaos property.

COARSE-GRAINING → SIGNAL / DETAIL TRADEOFF

What happens when many microstates become one macrostate?

Cₖ(x) = block-average of k neighboring samples

Increase the block size. Fine fluctuations disappear, broad structure can become easier to see, and some genuine information is irreversibly lost. Whether discarded variation is “noise” depends on the task.

Intelligence claim: coarse-graining is a powerful abstraction mechanism, but QEVA does not equate coarse-graining with intelligence. An intelligent system may need multiple resolutions, adaptive summaries, memory, objectives, and feedback.

LOCAL SEARCH ↔ EXPLORATION

Why greedy improvement can miss the best region

x[t+1] = x[t] + local_step + exploration_noise

The landscape contains multiple peaks. Pure local ascent can settle at the first nearby maximum. Controlled exploration can cross lower regions and sometimes discover a better basin.

No universal schedule: oscillating between local and global search can be useful, but the optimal balance depends on objective geometry, noise, information cost, and time horizon.
